I built this working ferris wheel for my week two assignment for How to Make Almost Anything (HTMAA) (6.9020). I wanted to learn about parametric design, [living hinges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_hinge) and wooden joints. Every piece was designed in Fusion 360, then laser cut and assembled. No glue anywhere.

## Designing the ferris wheel

I split the design into two parts:

1. The ferris wheel itself
2. The base

For the base, I wanted to see how the sides would bend, so I used Fusion 360's **Sheet Metal** tool. That let me fit the parts together while bent, then export a flattened DXF for laser cutting. I used a reference image for the living hinges and rectangular patterned it along the sides of the base.

![Base designed as bent sheet metal](./sheet-metal-box.png)
*Extruded sheet metal base*

For the wheel, I drew everything in sketches and extruded it. All the pieces have finger joints, and I used the **Joint** tool to check everything fitted together.

## Laser cutting the parts

Laser cutting means accounting for kerf. Thankfully there's a Fusion 360 plugin, [DXF For Laser](https://apps.autodesk.com/FUSION/en/Detail/Index?id=7634902334100976871&os=Win64&appLang=en), that exports **.dxf** files from your bodies' faces and offsets the dimensions for kerf.

I exported all my bodies, made some test cuts, then cut everything out. It took a few tries to get the kerf right, but in the end, with the help of a pallet, everything fitted together.
