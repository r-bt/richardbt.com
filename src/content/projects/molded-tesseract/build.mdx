This build was inspired by a the work of a [previous HTMAA student](http://fab.cba.mit.edu/classes/863.12/people/matted/weekly-projects/6-molding-and-casting.html) whose hypercube lived in the [The Deep](https://make.mit.edu/metropolis), a (now closed) makerspace where I ran a weekly silicone mold training. The hypercube was also a favourite for people to try mold in. However, the 3D printed mold was notoriously hard to get a good cube out of, so I decided to make my own for my trainings.

## The plan

1. CAD the hypercube in Fusion 360
2. Generate its negative
3. Cut the negative out of a block of wax on a router
4. Mold the cube in silicone

## Designing the tesseract

I started with two cubes in Fusion 360.

![Cube inside another cube](./cube-in-cube.png)
*Cube inside another cube*

I drew a line from a bottom corner to a top corner and made a pipe with a rectangular cross section along it.

![Cube with an arm](./cube-arm.png)
*Cube with an arm*

Circular patterning and mirroring the arm gave me all 8 structs.

![Finished cube](./cube-finished.png)
*Finished cube*

To mold it, I actually needed the negative of the space the hypercube takes up. I made a cube 10mm wider than mine, placed the hypercube inside, and used Fusion's **Combine** feature to cut it out.

![Cube with hypercube cut out](./cube-with-hypercube-cut-out.png)
*Cube with hypercube cut out*

Four planes, each at 45 degrees, divided the block into six pieces.

![Cube with 4 planes](./cube-with-planes.png)
*Cube with 4 planes*

That gave me the piece I needed to mold.

![Negative of cube to mold](./cube-negative.png)
*Negative of cube to mold*


## CAM and cutting

I was given a **1.5"x3"x7"** block of wax to cut on the ShopBot CNC router. In a new Fusion 360 file I modeled the block and cut the hypercube negative out of it.

![Wax block with cube cut out](./wax-with-cube-cutout.png)
*Wax with cube cutout*

In the manufacturing workspace I used an **adaptive clearing** and a **flat facing** before a contour finishing pass.

![Adaptive clearing toolpath](./adaptive-cam.png)
*Adaptive clearing operation*

Then I cut the block on the router.

![Wax block after being cut by the router](./cut-wax-block.jpg)
*Wax block after cutting*

## Molding the sides

Originally I planned to use Oomoo, however this material has a **cure time** of around 2 hours and as I only had one block I could only mold two sides at a time. Instead I went to The Deep, which had a different material _Mold Star_, which has a cure time of 30 minutes.

To work out how much material I needed, I filled the wax block with couscous and measured it.

![Couscous in the wax mold](./wax-block-with-cous-cous.jpg)
*Measuring volume with couscous*

I mixed Part A and Part B of Mold Star. As Mold Star has a pot time of only 6 minutes, there was no time for photos! After 30 minutes, out came the mold:

![Two molded cube sides](./two-molded-cube-sides.jpg)
*Two molded cube sides*

Two more rounds gave me all six pieces.

![Six molded cube sides](./six-molded-cube-sides.jpg)
*All six molded cube sides*

## Casting the cube

Now I could **finally** cast the hypercube. I applied **mold release** to each piece and held them together with elastic bands.

![Assembled mold held with elastic bands](./assembled-molded-cube.jpg)
*Assembled mold*

I mixed Part A and Part B of **Dragon Skin**, another silicone, with blue dye and some glitter for effect and degassed it in a vacuum chamber.

![Casting material beside the mold](./casting-material.jpg)
*Casting material beside the mold*

I poured it into the mold, and 24 hours later I took out my cube. It worked!
