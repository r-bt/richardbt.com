I'm one of the TAs for [ES.100](http://student.mit.edu/catalog/search.cgi?search=ES.100), Introduction to Maker Skills. In 2023 we got funding to run an LED neon sign workshop, so I made an Appa sign to test the process.

## Designing the sign

I found a good image of Appa and used Adobe Illustrator to convert it into a vector of just lines. Then I offset all the paths to make the outline of the sign. The LED strips come in 1in increments, so I designed the sign around that constraint.

## Setting up the CAM

I brought the file into Fusion 360 and set up the CAM, using a 1/8" endmill to cut the sign from a 1/4" sheet of acrylic.

![Simulated CAM job in Fusion 360](./cam-preview.png)
*Simulated CAM job in Fusion 360*

## Cutting the sign

I cut the sign on the ShopBot CNC router.

![Sign being cut on a CNC router](./router-job.jpg)
*Sign being cut on the CNC router*

## Wiring the sign

This was the hardest part. I cut the LED strips to fit the different channels, soldered them together, and wrapped the exposed wires in heat shrink to prevent shorts.

![LED strips and wires laid into the sign's channels](./adding-wires.jpg)
*Adding the wires to the channels*

The copper pads on the strips were so small that I used hot glue as mechanical stress relief.

Finally, I connected the sign to a 12V power supply and it lit up!
